Tracing gene regulation in Alzheimer’s disease.

A multi-omics graph-learning approach to nonlinear regulatory pathways.

Xi’an Jiaotong University · Undergraduate thesisDec 2025–Jun 2026

The question

How can we infer distal, nonlinear regulatory pathways in Alzheimer’s disease, rather than relying only on linear associations between nearby genomic features?

Connecting transcription factors, chromatin, and genes

For my undergraduate thesis, I integrated paired hippocampal single-nucleus ATAC-seq and RNA-seq data from 50,381 cells into an explicit TF → Peak → Gene regulatory graph. The graph combines JASPAR/FIMO motif scanning with distance-based genomic priors.

Figure forthcomingTF → Peak → Gene model and evaluation figure

My contributions

  • Integrated the two molecular modalities and constructed the regulatory graph.
  • Designed a two-stage, eight-head graph attention network with a differentiable ATAC-prediction gate and a multitask objective.
  • Evaluated predictive performance and regulatory interpretability through baseline comparisons, ablations, perturbations, and GO enrichment.

Outcome and interpretation

The thesis was completed with a defence score of 97/100. Gene-expression prediction achieved R² = 0.9904 in the thesis evaluation.

Evaluation figures and details of the train/test protocol will be added here. Predictive performance and biological interpretation are assessed separately; prediction accuracy alone does not establish a causal regulatory mechanism.

Earlier work in computational biology

Before this thesis, I contributed to knowledge-graph research for circRNA–disease association prediction (KGRACDA), and to data-processing experiments and simulations for polyploid genome-size estimation (findGSEP). These projects led to two co-authored publications in 2024.
